# Detection methods (overview)

Different methods are sensitive to different planets.

Use this page to map **a discovery claim** to **what was actually measured**.

### The big five

* **Transits**: planet crosses the star. Measures radius.
* **Radial velocity (RV)**: star’s Doppler wobble. Measures $$M\sin i$$.
* **Direct imaging**: planet light separated from star. Measures brightness + spectrum.
* **Microlensing**: gravity acts like a lens. Measures mass ratio and projected separation.
* **Astrometry**: star’s position wobble on the sky. Measures true mass.

### Bonus methods you’ll see

* **Timing**: pulsar timing, eclipse timing, and transit timing variations (TTVs).
* **Disk structure**: gaps/spirals in protoplanetary disks (indirect evidence).

### Which planets each method likes

* Transits: short-period planets and small stars.
* RV: massive planets and quiet stars.
* Imaging: young, hot, wide-orbit giants.
* Microlensing: cold planets at a few AU, often far away.
* Astrometry: massive planets on wider orbits around nearby stars.

{% hint style="info" %}
Most “nice” planets have **multiple** methods involved. Discovery + follow-up is the norm.
{% endhint %}
