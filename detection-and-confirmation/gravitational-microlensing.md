# Gravitational microlensing

Microlensing happens when a foreground star passes in front of a background star. Gravity focuses the background light.

A planet around the lens star adds a short anomaly.

### What microlensing measures best

* Planet-to-star mass ratio.
* Projected separation (in Einstein radii).

### Why it’s powerful

* Sensitive to cold planets.
* Works at kiloparsec distances.
* Can detect low-mass planets in some cases.

### Why follow-up is hard

* Events are one-time.
* Host stars can be faint.
* Orbits are usually not fully determined.

{% hint style="info" %}
Microlensing gives demographics. It’s less suited for detailed characterization.
{% endhint %}
