# Transit photometry

A **transit** is a dip in starlight when a planet crosses the star.

### What transits measure directly

* Transit depth $$\rightarrow$$ $$R_p/R_\star$$.
* Period $$P$$ from repeat events.
* Impact parameter and duration $$\rightarrow$$ geometry constraints.

### What you can infer (with assumptions)

* Absolute radius $$R_p$$ needs a stellar radius.
* Semi-major axis $$a$$ uses the stellar mass.
* Bulk density needs an independent mass.

### Common transit false positives

* Eclipsing binaries.
* Background blended binaries.
* Starspots and stellar variability.
* Instrument systematics.

### Typical follow-up

1. Check centroid shifts and odd/even depth.
2. High-resolution imaging (rule out blends).
3. RVs for mass or upper limits.

{% hint style="warning" %}
A single transit is interesting, not definitive. Long-period planets often need years of follow-up.
{% endhint %}
