# What is an exoplanet?

An **exoplanet** is a planet that orbits a star other than the Sun.

That’s the common-use meaning. It’s also what most catalogs use.

### What counts as a “planet” in practice

Most exoplanet catalogs follow a pragmatic rule:

* If it **orbits a star** and looks planet-like.
* If it’s below the **brown dwarf regime** (fuzzy boundary).
* If the discovery paper calls it a planet.

The messy bit is the planet–brown dwarf boundary.

### Edge cases you’ll see

* **Brown dwarfs**: objects between planets and stars. They can look like giant planets.
* **Free-floating planets**: planet-mass objects not bound to a star. Often found via microlensing.
* **Circumbinary planets**: planets orbiting two stars.

### Candidate vs confirmed

A transit dip is not a planet by default.

* **Candidate**: signal consistent with a planet.
* **Confirmed**: mass-confirmed or statistically validated.
